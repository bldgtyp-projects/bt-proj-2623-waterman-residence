In Pittsburgh, window design balances useful winter sun against summer heat. Rooms with a lot of south-
or west-facing glass need the most care.

All radiation results include the neighboring houses, modeled as simple blocks from
[OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/) and [CadMapper](https://cadmapper.com/) footprints, the
two rear-yard trees, and the surveyed ground and lot lines. Neighbor heights are approximate.

1. **Close neighbors on both sides.** The side yards are only 4 to 5 ft wide. The three-story house at
   6914 McPherson and the two-story house at 6922 block much of the sun at the lower side windows,
   especially in winter.
2. **Orientation.** The street facade faces north-northeast and the garden south-southwest. The rear
   gets the most winter sun, and the most summer and afternoon heat.
3. **Rear-yard trees.** The two large trees south of the house shade the lower rear windows. They are
   modeled as solid, so winter shading from the bare trees is somewhat overstated.
4. **Porches, balcony and overhangs.** The porches and second-floor balcony shade the glass beneath
   them all year. That helps in summer and costs solar gain in winter.

The upper floors are well above the neighbors. They get the most winter sun and the most summer heat.
