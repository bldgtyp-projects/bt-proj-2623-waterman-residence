In summer, the upper west-side windows and some sunroom doors get the most sun. Shade those first. The
rear bay window is shaded by the balcony, but the upper rear glazing in Katy's Studio is not.

The sunroom and third-floor rooms are the most likely to overheat, which we also see in the Resilience simulations.

**We recommend interior blinds at south-facing windows**, as well as exterior shading at exposed south- and southwest-facing and other high-sun openings wherever practical. Exterior shades stop the heat before it reaches the glass. Choose interior blinds by their rated solar performance.

All windows use SHGC 0.30. Different glass by orientation is an option if the shading isn't enough, though this complicates the window order.

Night ventilation through open windows can cool the house when it's cooler outside, but can also bring
in moisture. Any automatic window control should consider temperature, humidity and security.
