In any high-performance building, both the energy and the comfort impacts of the glazing must be carefully assessed.
The comfort evaluation looks at overall comfort (air temperature and relative humidity) and also at the local
discomfort caused by cold surfaces and drafts near the glass.

In particular, the localized discomfort caused by radiant temperature asymmetry (a difference in the temperature of the
surfaces around the body) decides whether perimeter heating can be left out. If the asymmetry is too large, a
compensating heat source, typically a radiator or baseboard under the window, is needed to offset the discomfort.

Engineering reference standards suggest that ideal comfort is found when the surfaces around a person differ by less
than 7.6°F [4.2 K]. The windows matter most here, because they are where the coldest interior surfaces occur in winter.

For Pittsburgh's winter design conditions [ <Var k="narrative.climate.ashrae_winter_design_temp_F"/>°F
(<Var k="narrative.climate.ashrae_winter_design_temp_C"/>°C), ASHRAE 99.6% ], Passive House comfort guidance
recommends windows with an **installed U-value below <Var k="narrative.windows.comfort_target_u_value" />
Btu/hr-ft²-F** [ R-value above <Var k="narrative.windows.comfort_target_r_value" /> hr-ft²-F/Btu ]. Windows at
that level keep interior glass and frame surfaces warm enough that no perimeter heat is needed. Smaller windows, which
occupy less of a person's field of view, can meet the comfort target at slightly higher U-values.

No window has been selected yet; the architect's narrative calls for triple-pane tilt-turn windows with the

- A code-level window does **not** meet the comfort target in this climate. The large living-room, sunroom and
  stair glazing would need perimeter heat.
- We recommend installed whole-window U-values of about **U-<Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" />**
  [ R-<Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_r_value" /> ], which clears the comfort target with margin. That is a
  good-quality triple-pane window with an insulated frame, installed with the frame overlapping the exterior
  insulation.
- Whole-window performance data is needed, not center-of-glass values. The frame, the spacer and the way the window
  sits in the 4" of exterior insulation (the installation psi-value) all count.
- Specify the solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC) by orientation: higher on the south for winter gain, lower on the
  west and on unshaded upper-floor glass to limit summer heat.

Window comfort is the reason to resolve the window specification early: it sets the window budget, the
installation detail at the 4" exterior insulation, and whether perimeter heat has to be designed into the
mechanical system.
