This report evaluates the energy performance, thermal comfort, resilience and long-term durability of the
new Waterman Residence in Pittsburgh's Point Breeze North neighborhood, designed by
[mossArchitects](https://www.mossarc.com/). We tested the house in five design variants, from a
code-minimum baseline, through the house as currently drawn, to a package that meets the
**Phius CORE 2024** performance targets. The pages that follow document the modeling assumptions, the
results and our recommendations.

### Designed for the climate the house will live in

Every result in this report is calculated against a projected **mid-century climate** (SSP2-4.5, 2040 to
2059) built for this site, not against historical weather. A house built now will spend most of its life,
and its heat pumps all of theirs, in a warmer and more humid Pittsburgh. The method was proposed by the
owners and is set out on the [Future Climate](/future-climate/) page. In this climate the year averages
about 5.4°F warmer than the standard Phius data for Pittsburgh: winters are milder, and summers are hotter
and noticeably more humid.

### Phius as a design guide, not a certification

The owners are not pursuing Passive House certification. We use the **Phius CORE 2024** criteria as the
yardstick because they are calculated for this specific building: its size, its occupancy and its climate.
They give a clear, defensible answer to the question "how good is good enough?" without the paperwork,
fees and third-party verification of a certification.

The architect's stated targets, a HERS Index of 25 or lower and airtightness of 2.0 ACH50 or better, sit
alongside this analysis. The HERS rating is handled separately by the owners' rater; the Phius targets in
this report are stricter on airtightness and heating, so a house that meets them should have no difficulty
with the HERS goal.

### What the model shows

- **The envelope as drawn is a good starting point.** The As-Drawn package cuts total site energy by
  about a quarter compared with code minimum, and heating demand by nearly half.
- **Airtightness and ventilation are the largest remaining levers.** The steps from As-Drawn to the Phius
  2024 package change no insulation at all: tighter air sealing (3.0 to 1.0 ACH50), better junction
  details and a high-efficiency ERV. Together they cut site energy by another 31%, to about 17,300 kWh/yr,
  roughly half of the code-minimum house.
- **The Phius 2024 package meets all four Phius space-conditioning targets**, but the peak heating load
  passes by about 1%. That margin depends on the window selection, which is still open.
- **Cooling and dehumidification are now half as large as heating.** In the mid-century climate, moisture
  removal is a real load. The ERV's moisture recovery is worth as much here as its heat recovery, and the
  heat pumps should be selected for dehumidification, not only for temperature.

### What we recommend

Build to the **Phius 2024** package: the assemblies as drawn, 1.0 ACH50 airtightness verified by
blower-door testing, a high-efficiency ERV with moisture recovery, careful thermal-bridge detailing,
and high-performance triple-pane windows. Confirm the sloped-roof insulation with the design team, since
the roof as modeled is the weakest assembly on the house. **Do not pursue Phius certification**; use its
targets to guide the design.

### Resilience

Separately from the annual energy model, the house will be stress-tested against the **Phius REVIVE 2024**
seven-day power-outage protocol, for a summer heat wave and a winter cold snap, using the future-climate
weather file. Results will be posted on the [Resilience](/resilience/) page.
