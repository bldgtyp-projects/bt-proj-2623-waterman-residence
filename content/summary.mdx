This report compares five design options for the Waterman Residence in Pittsburgh, designed by
[mossArchitects](https://www.mossarc.com/). It covers annual energy use, window comfort and how the
house holds up during a week-long power outage. It is a design study, not a HERS rating or a
certification assessment.

### Our recommendation

Develop the **Phius 2024** option. Target **1.0 ACH50** airtightness, verified by blower-door tests,
and use a high-efficiency ERV. Choose windows for winter comfort and shade the most exposed openings.

Keep the batt-insulated roof with an interior smart membrane, ADHERO over the plywood and a ventilated
cavity under the metal roofing. We prefer exterior basement insulation if the team can resolve the
finish detail.

The option is named for the Phius benchmark it was tested against. The owners are not pursuing Phius
certification, and the package does not meet every Phius criterion.

### What the annual model shows

- The recommended option uses about **23,610 kWh/year**, **28% less than As-Drawn**. That includes
  household loads, steam humidification and an elevator allowance.
- It is below all four space-conditioning targets. Peak heating is about **24,400 Btu/hour**,
  **16% below** the target.
- Heating uses about **5,650 kWh/year**; cooling and dehumidification use about **3,500**. Choose
  equipment that can remove moisture when the house needs little cooling.
- Holding **35% winter RH** with steam humidification uses about **2,350 kWh/year**. Check window
  condensation at that humidity before choosing equipment.

Outdoor heating and EV charging are not yet in the totals.

### Future climate and outages

The annual model uses projected mid-century weather, **SSP2-4.5 for 2040 to 2059**, and keeps
historical winter conditions for heating loads. See [Future Climate](/future-climate/).

In a week-long outage with heating and cooling off and the ERV running at low speed on backup power,
summer Heat Index peaks at about **100°F** and winter indoor air falls to about **39°F**. See
[Resilience](/resilience/). These results are preliminary. Use them to decide what backup power
should cover, not to size equipment.

### Next design choices

- Foundation insulation location and finish detail, and foam-free products for the slab and low-slope roof.
- Window products from supplier quotes, with SHGC 0.30 throughout. We recommend interior blinds at
  south-facing windows and exterior shading at exposed high-solar openings.
- Outdoor heating use, and elevator supplier data.
- Backup-power scope: which rooms and systems it serves. Equipment and battery sizes follow from that.

The owners' **HERS Index target of 25 or lower** needs a separate rating by their rater. The architect
calls for 2.0 ACH50 or better, with 1.0 preferred; we recommend 1.0.
