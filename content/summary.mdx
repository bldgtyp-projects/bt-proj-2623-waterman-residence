This report is a design-phase energy analysis of the Waterman Residence in Pittsburgh, designed by
[mossArchitects](https://www.mossarc.com/). In order to assess building performance across a range of axes, we built a whole-building energy model of the house and then used this model to test six distinct design options. The results are meant to guide decisions on the envelope, windows and mechanical systems while the design is still flexible.

### Our recommendation

We recommend developing the **Phius 2024** option, named for the [Phius](https://www.phius.org/phius-certification-guidebook) passive-building
targets it was tested against. It combines very tight construction, better windows and a high-efficiency ventilation system with heat and moisture recovery.

### Key findings

- **Energy use.** The high-performance 'Phius 2024' variant uses about **29% less electricity** than the house as drawn. See  [Site energy](/energy_model/#site-energy).
- **Heating and cooling.** The 'Phius 2024' variant needs about **half the peak heating** of the house as drawn, which means smaller equipment. See [Energy targets](/energy_model/#passive-house-thresholds).
- **Power outages.** Even in the 'Phius 2024' scenario, during a week-long winter power outage the home has trouble maintaining safe interior conditions for the entire week. In summer, heat and humidity make the upper floors uncomfortable quickly, although the basement stays relatively safe. Deciding what backup power should cover is one of the main open questions. See [Resilience](/resilience/) for more information and details.

### Next Steps

The biggest open choices have to do with assembly insulation types, and which window type to use. Each section includes decisions to be discussed with the team in order to decide the appropriate direction for the design.
