#### Kitchen venting

{/* TODO(ed): PHPP models the hood at 624 cfm (Addl vent). Confirm hood selection with the architect. */}

The draft model carries a kitchen range hood of about <Var k="narrative.user_defined.kitchen_hood_cfm" /> CFM. On an
induction or electric range that is more than the house needs, and above 400 CFM the code requires a dedicated make-up air
system ([IRC M1503.6](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IRC2021P3/chapter-15-exhaust-systems)). Choosing a well-designed
hood at 400 CFM or less avoids that requirement altogether, and it is our first recommendation.

Kitchen venting should be provided at the lowest rate that satisfies regulations. For gas cooking, this flow rate will
be specified by code. For electric cooking, limit exhaust hood airflow to 200-400 cfm maximum through the selection of
an appropriately sized hood. The Home Ventilating Institute (HVI) specifies a minimum of 40 cfm and recommends 100 cfm
per linear foot of range. For effective kitchen venting, sizing and placement of the hood can be more important than the
air flow rate, as sizing and placement affect the capture efficiency of the hood. Hoods should be as low as practical,
extend beyond the range by approximately six inches at the sides and be as deep as possible without interfering with use
of the range.

#### Makeup air systems

In order to properly vent any exhaust air appliances (clothes dryers, kitchen hoods, etc.) in an air-tight home, a
dedicated makeup air system may be necessary. This is especially true of homes with combustion appliances (e.g. wood
stoves/fireplaces) located indoors. In this scenario, makeup air from an automatic makeup air fan is used to balance out
the exhausted air.

Note that for most cases we do NOT recommend this strategy as it is costly, complicated, and adds considerable
complexity to the envelope construction (insulation, air-sealing) and represents a significant energy penalty to
heat/cool the makeup air.

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <span>
    Wherever possible, the use of all-electric systems should be considered, and combustion appliances should be
    installed only in outdoor locations (porches, decks, etc.).
  </span>
</div>

Heating or cooling of outdoor makeup air may be needed once it enters the habitable space. Self-closing (magnetic)
dampers should be included on all ducting to prevent air infiltration / exfiltration when the appliances are not in use.
In order not to compromise occupant comfort when makeup air is being supplied to the home, a high output heating element
will need to be sized and installed to heat the incoming volume of air to a comfortable temperature.

There are several good, self-modulating makeup air systems (MUAS) and corresponding makeup air heaters (MUAH) which could
be a good fit for this project:

- [Electro Industries](https://electromn.com/make-up-air-2/)
- [Neptronic](https://www.neptronic.com/cmu/)

#### Makeup air system commissioning

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <span>
    Proper commissioning and continued maintenance of the makeup air system is critical to the safe operation of the
    home, particularly when combustion appliances are present.
  </span>
</div>

Several things should be kept in mind when configuring such a system:

1. Ensure airtightness at all envelope penetrations.
2. Insulate all ducting with vapor-closed insulation (min. 2") and seal all joints with vapor-closed tape.
3. Provide no more than 60% of the make-up air at the base of the range/oven.
4. For gas cooking, follow all local code requirements and the manufacturer's instructions.

#### Other loads in the program

The architect's narrative includes several systems that run every year for the life of the house and are worth
deciding on their own merits:

- **Steam humidifier.** The ERV's moisture recovery keeps winter humidity up on its own in most airtight houses. We
  suggest monitoring the first winter (see [Building Monitoring](#building-monitoring)) before committing to a
  humidifier, or at least sizing it from measured data.
- **Whole-house dehumidifier.** Pittsburgh's humid summers make dedicated dehumidification worthwhile, especially in
  the basement. Choose a unit that can run without overcooling the house in the shoulder seasons.
- **Hot-water recirculation loop.** Run it on demand (push button or occupancy sensor) rather than continuously, and
  insulate the loop fully. A continuously running loop can use more energy than the hot water it delivers.
- **Heated screened-porch and hardscape slabs.** Both sit outside the thermal envelope and heat the outdoors. If they
  stay, put them on controls that run only when snow or ice is present.
