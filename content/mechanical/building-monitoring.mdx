### Indoor conditions

Put temperature and humidity sensors on each floor, including the basement and the third-floor rooms.
Review the first winter and summer to tune the controls and find rooms that run warm or damp. A CO₂
sensor in a bedroom or living space shows how well the ventilation keeps up.

Choose sensors with documented accuracy and downloadable history. Cloud-connected sensors stop
reporting when the internet or power goes out; if outage data matters, pick ones that log locally on
backup power. [Netatmo](https://www.netatmo.com/) and [WirelessTag](https://wirelesstag.net/) are two
to look at.

### Electricity use

Circuit monitoring catches unexpected heating, hot-water or standby loads. Plan it with the panel
design, especially for the heat pumps, water heater and outdoor heating. Options include a panel with
built-in monitoring, such as [Leviton](https://leviton.com/products/residential/load-centers/the-leviton-smart-load-center),
or add-on meters such as [Emporia](https://www.emporiaenergy.com/) and [eGauge](https://www.egauge.net/).
The electrician should confirm compatibility.
