For this house we recommend a high-performance ERV with better than 80% rated heat recovery and 60% or better moisture
recovery. The <VarLink hrefKey="narrative.mechanical.erv.link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.manufacturer_name" /> ComfoAir Q</VarLink>
named in the architect's narrative is a good selection. Its enthalpy-exchanger version provides the moisture recovery,
and its MERV 13 filtration keeps the indoor air clean. Size the unit
for the high-speed airflow of <Var k="narrative.user_defined.design_airflow_cfm" /> CFM while running it at its
normal speed most of the time; a unit running well below its maximum is quieter and uses less fan energy.

The **effective** heat recovery in the energy model runs lower than the unit's rated figure, because it accounts for
heat lost from the outdoor-air and exhaust ducts. Locate the ERV so that those two ducts are as short as physically
possible, and insulate them with vapor-closed insulation. The architect's narrative leaves the unit location open,
between the basement mechanical room and an upper-floor closet. A basement location close to an outside wall keeps the
two outdoor ducts short; an upper-floor location shortens the supply runs to the bedrooms. Either can work if the
outdoor ducts stay short.

The values in this analysis are assumptions. <Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.manufacturer_name" /> or a qualified
ventilation designer should be retained to design, specify and balance the system after installation. This matters
for proper operation and is standard practice on high-performance houses. Zehnder's design and balancing service
normally comes with its packages, but confirm this for this project.

#### System balancing

Before occupancy, the fresh-air system should be tested and balanced to ensure good air mixing and adequate supply to
all living spaces. Measured supply and extract airflows must agree within 10% of each other.
