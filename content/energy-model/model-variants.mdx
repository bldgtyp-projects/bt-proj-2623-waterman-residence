We compared six options in the results below:
- **Code Minimum** uses Pennsylvania's amended 2021 whole-assembly U-values for climate zone 5. See the [adopted U-factor table](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/034/chapter403/s403.21.html).
- **Moderate Envelope** sets each assembly R-value about halfway between Code Minimum and As-Drawn,
  keeps the code-minimum windows and airtightness, and adds an HRV, with a +15% thermal-bridge allowance.
- **As-Drawn** uses the assemblies on the [Envelope](/building_envelope/) page and an HRV.
- **Improved Envelope** adds better windows and tighter air sealing, and cuts the thermal-bridge
  allowance from +15% to only +10%.
- **Improved Mech.** switches to a higher-performing ERV, which adds moisture recovery.
- **Phius 2024**, named for the [Phius CORE 2024](https://www.phius.org/phius-certification-guidebook) targets, reaches 1.0 ACH50, better windows and a higher-efficiency ERV, with a +5% thermal-bridge allowance.

**Thermal-bridge allowance.** The 'thermal bridges' at junctions between assemblies, such as wall-to-floor and wall-to-roof, lose extra heat that can only be calculated once the construction details are fully drawn. Until
then the model carries an allowance for this extra heat loss, expressed as a percentage added to the house's total heat loss. During construction documents each junction will be calculated and charged against that allowance. A smaller
allowance in the variants here indicates more attention from the design team during CDs: catalog and calculate every junction, and refine the details until they fit.

**Airtightness.** The ACH50 values are targets we give the model. Air leakage can't be simulated
from drawings; only a blower-door test measures it. A lower target asks for more care in the
air-barrier details and on site. See [Air sealing](/building_envelope/#building-airtightness).

Every option uses heat pumps and steam humidification to 35% winter RH. None includes PV or an elevator; the shaft is built now and the elevator, if added later, will be a future load.

### Occupancy

The model carries **three occupants** as a continuous average presence, the way PHPP treats a home:
their body heat, hot water and appliance use are spread over the year rather than scheduled hour by
hour. Internal heat gains use PHPP's residential standard, and hot-water, appliance and plug loads
follow the [Phius 2024 calculator](https://www.phius.org/phius-certification-guidebook) defaults for a house of this size with two bedrooms. Guests and work-from-home days are inside that average.
