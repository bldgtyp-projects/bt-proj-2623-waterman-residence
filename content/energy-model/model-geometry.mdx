For all model results shown here, the following building geometry was used. This geometry is based on the
mossArchitects drawings and Revit model received on **<Var k="source_files.cad_files_received_date" />**. The model
includes both the building and the local shading context: the surrounding houses as simple massing blocks,
including the neighbors only a few feet from each side wall, and the two large trees in the rear yards. For details on the context shading see the
[windows site shading section](/windows/#site-shading).

The modeled house measures about 56 ft deep by 37 ft wide, and rises about 47 ft from the basement floor to the
main ridge. The house is <Var k="narrative.user_defined.stories" /> on a <Var k="narrative.user_defined.lot_size" /> infill
lot, with a one-story garage at the rear. The garage, the front porch and the screened porch sit outside the
thermal envelope. The basement is inside it: it is heated, ventilated and counted in the floor area.

The modeled interior conditioned floor area (iCFA) is <Var k="narrative.user_defined.icfa_ft2" /> ft², wrapped by
<Var k="narrative.user_defined.envelope_area_ft2" /> ft² of thermal enclosure. That works out to roughly
**<Var k="narrative.user_defined.envelope_to_icfa_ratio" /> ft² of enclosure per ft² of floor area**, a
reasonable figure for a house of this size and height. Compact plans land closer to 2.0; spread-out
single-story houses often exceed 4.0.

The geometry is not as simple as the ratio suggests, however. Several roof planes, dormers, the bay window,
the balcony and the junction with the garage each add corners and edges to the enclosure. Those are the
places where heat bypasses the insulation (thermal bridges), and where the air barrier depends on field
workmanship. Both are discussed in the [Envelope](/building_envelope/) section.
