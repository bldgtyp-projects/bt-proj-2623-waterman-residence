Total annual energy use, shown in the [Site Energy](#site-energy) section, is the headline figure. The Phius
framework adds four more targets: annual heating demand, annual cooling demand, and the peak heating and
cooling loads. The peak loads matter most for the mechanical design, because they set the size of the
heat pumps, and a smaller system is cheaper to buy and runs more efficiently.

For **Phius CORE 2024** these limits are calculated specifically for this building by the
Phius Criteria Calculator (<Var k="narrative.user_defined.phius_calculator_version" />),
from its enclosure area, floor area, bedroom count and climate. For this house they are listed below. The calculator
uses the standard climate; we hold its limits as the targets for the mid-century results (see
[Climate Data](#climate-data)).

| Criterion | Phius CORE 2024 limit |
| --- | --- |
| Annual heating demand | <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_hd_limit" /> kBtu/ft²·yr |
| Annual cooling demand | <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_cd_limit" /> kBtu/ft²·yr |
| Peak heating load | <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_hl_limit" /> Btu/hr·ft² |
| Peak cooling load | <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_cl_limit" /> Btu/hr·ft² |
| Net source energy | <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_nse_limit" /> kWh/person·yr |
| Airtightness | <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_cfm50_limit" /> CFM50/ft² of enclosure |

Because this house is not seeking certification, these are design targets, not pass/fail requirements.
Where the house misses one, that tells us where improvement is possible and roughly how much it would
take. Shown below are the results for each variant.

### Results

| Variant | Heating demand (kBtu/ft²·yr) | Cooling demand (kBtu/ft²·yr) | Peak heating (Btu/hr·ft²) | Peak cooling (Btu/hr·ft²) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Phius CORE 2024 limit | 8.6 | 5.9 | 5.9 | 2.1 |
| Code Minimum | 21.6 | 4.4 | 15.2 | 2.7 |
| As-Drawn | 11.7 | 4.2 | 10.5 | 2.1 |
| Improved Envelope | 9.5 | 4.0 | 8.3 | 2.0 |
| Improved Mech. | 9.3 | 3.8 | 8.2 | 2.0 |
| **Phius 2024** | **7.2** | **3.5** | **5.85** | **1.9** |

Annual cooling demand is not the difficult target: every variant, even code minimum, is inside it. Heating is where
the variants separate. The As-Drawn house uses a little under half the heating of the code-minimum house, but still
about 35% more than the Phius target. Improved airtightness and the ERV close most of that gap, and only the full
Phius 2024 package clears it, with a margin of about 17%.

The peak loads are the tighter targets. The Phius 2024 package meets the peak heating limit by about 1% and the
peak cooling limit by about 12%. The As-Drawn house sits right at the peak cooling limit; the intermediate variants
fall just under it.

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <strong>Design implication</strong>
  <span>
    A 1% margin on peak heating does not survive much change, and the largest open input is the window. Every
    variant still carries a code-level U-0.30 window. A high-performance triple-pane window, as recommended in
    the <a href="/windows/#window-thermal-comfort">Window Thermal Comfort</a> section, should open up a comfortable
    margin on both heating targets. Peak heating also sets the size of the heat pumps: at about 29,000 Btu/hr for the
    whole house, the Phius 2024 package needs a little over half the heating capacity of the As-Drawn house (about 52,000 Btu/hr).
