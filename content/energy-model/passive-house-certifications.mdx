The owners have chosen not to certify this house. The **Phius CORE 2024** targets are used throughout this
report as the measure of a high-performance house in this climate. For reference, if the team ever reconsidered,
certification would add the following beyond the modeled performance:

- **Airtightness testing.** A measured blower-door result of no more than
  <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_cfm50_limit" /> CFM50 per ft² of enclosure area. On this house's
  <Var k="narrative.user_defined.envelope_area_ft2" /> ft² enclosure, that is close to the 1.0 ACH50
  recommended here, so the recommended package already aims at it.
- **Window comfort and condensation checks**, window by window, against the specified product. See
  [Window Thermal Comfort](/windows/#window-thermal-comfort).
- **Thermal-bridge modeling.** Each significant junction calculated individually rather than covered by a
  percentage allowance.
- **Required personnel**: a Phius Certified Consultant (CPHC), a Phius Certified Rater or Verifier, and a
  Phius Certified Builder.
- **Co-requisite programs**: ENERGY STAR and the DOE Zero Energy Ready Home program, including their
  checklists and HERS rating. The owners' HERS rater would already cover part of this.
- **Fees and review rounds**: design review, construction-phase testing and verification, and final review.

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <span>
    None of these change how the house performs. They document and verify it. Without certification, the
    two items worth keeping are the blower-door test at 1.0 ACH50, written into the construction documents
    as a contractual requirement, and careful detailing of the junctions identified in the
    <a href="/building_envelope/">Envelope</a> section.
  </span>
</div>
