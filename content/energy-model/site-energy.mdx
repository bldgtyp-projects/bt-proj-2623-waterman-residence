Site energy is the electricity delivered to the house: heat pumps, hot water, appliances and every
other modeled load. Heating demand, by contrast, is the heat the building needs before equipment
efficiency.

| Option | Site energy (kWh/year) | Change from As-Drawn |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Code Minimum, code-value reference | 36,660 | 12% more |
| As-Drawn | 32,700 | Reference |
| Improved Envelope | 29,250 | 11% less |
| Improved Mech. | 28,330 | 13% less |
| **Phius 2024** | **23,610** | **28% less** |

The recommended case saves about **9,090 kWh/year** against As-Drawn, from better windows, tighter air
sealing, fewer thermal bridges and better ventilation recovery.

### Where the electricity goes

In the recommended case, for three occupants:

- Heating: about **5,650 kWh/year**.
- Cooling and dehumidification: about **3,500 kWh/year**.
- Hot water: about **3,240 kWh/year**.
- Appliances, cooking, lighting and plug loads: about **6,960 kWh/year**.
- Steam humidification to **35% winter RH**: about **2,350 kWh/year**. The ERV slows winter drying
  but cannot hold that humidity on its own.
- Elevator: an allowance of **1,910 kWh/year**, from the smallest hydraulic-elevator bracket in the
  [Phius multifamily calculator](https://www.phius.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/Phius_Multifamily_Calculator_v24.0.2_2024.11.xlsx).
  Replace it with the supplier's operating, standby and cab-lighting data.

The charts' auxiliary category holds the humidifier and elevator.

### Loads still to define

Outdoor slab heating and EV charging are not in the totals. Define how the owners will use them, and
choose the hot-water recirculation controls, before reading these figures as a household budget.
