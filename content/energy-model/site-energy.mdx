"Site Energy" represents the energy purchased by the building and delivered to the site by the utility. This is
the most typical energy use figure assessed when considering a site "Net-Zero" building energy balance, or when
considering the annual cost of energy for the building. The site-energy total is made up of six main groups:
heating, cooling and dehumidification, hot water, lighting, electric equipment (appliances, electric vehicles and
so on), and pumps and fans.

To assess the performance of the home across a range of options, we have simulated five variants with different
energy-efficiency measures (see the [Model Variants](#model-variants) section for the inputs). All variants are
modeled as all-electric.

| Variant | Total site energy (kWh/yr) | Change from code minimum |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Code Minimum | 33,980 | |
| As-Drawn | 25,310 | −26% |
| Improved Envelope | 22,490 | −34% |
| Improved Mech. | 21,610 | −36% |
| **Phius 2024** | **17,340** | **−49%** |

The As-Drawn assemblies do the first, large piece of work. From there, the Phius 2024 package saves another 31%
without adding any insulation: the savings come from airtightness, junction detailing and the ERV. Fan and
auxiliary electricity drops by about half along the way, from roughly 4,100 to 2,100 kWh/yr, as the ERV and the
tighter envelope reduce the work the ventilation and dehumidification equipment has to do.

Cooling and dehumidification are a large share of the total in this climate: about 3,700 kWh/yr in the Phius 2024
package, against about 7,100 kWh/yr for heating. That is the mid-century climate showing up. Warmer, more humid
summers put real load on the heat pumps, and the moisture recovery in the ERV is one of the most effective ways to
reduce it.

Heating is the largest end use in every variant, and it is where most of the difference between the variants comes
from. Hot water, appliances and plug loads stay effectively constant at about 4,500 kWh/yr, so as the envelope
improves they become a larger share of the total: about a quarter of the Phius 2024 package's site energy. Past a certain point appliance
selection, lighting design and household habits move the utility bill more than further insulation does.

This program also carries several loads that a typical house does not: an elevator, whole-house
humidification and dehumidification, a hot-water recirculation loop, and a heated screened-porch and
hardscape slab. Each runs every year for the life of the house. They are worth reviewing on their own
merits, independent of the envelope decisions in this report.
