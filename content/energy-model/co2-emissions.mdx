Carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases released by energy use are the main driver of the warming of
the earth's atmosphere and oceans. Reducing those emissions means reducing them across buildings,
industry and transportation. [One useful benchmark](https://decarb.co/what-is-the-average-carbon-footprint-per-person/) suggests that by 2050 each person will need to keep to an
annual "carbon budget" of roughly 2-2.5 tons-CO<sub>2e</sub> for all activities: roughly 1 ton for housing and
building use, 1 ton for transportation and 0.5 tons for food. For scale, a single US-to-Europe round-trip
flight releases about 1-2 tons of CO<sub>2</sub>.

supplied to the house. Construction materials and equipment manufacture are not included in the totals shown below.

The recommended Phius 2024 package cuts emissions **29%** from As-Drawn and about a third from code minimum,
matching its electricity saving. However, 8.7 tons of CO<sub>2e</sub> is still well above the <Var k="narrative.co2.target_tons" />-ton benchmark target for a home with this level of occupancy. No envelope package can close the remaining gap on this grid.

RFC West still generates a large share of its electricity from coal and natural gas, which drives much of this high level of emissions. Its emission rate is about 1.5 times that of eastern Pennsylvania (RFCE, 599 lb/MWh), so each kWh saved here avoids more carbon emissions than it would in Philadelphia, New York or California. The grid rate is held constant in these figures, but the grid is getting cleaner over time, and because this house is all-electric its operating emissions will fall along with it.
