All of the modeled variants in this report use a **mid-century future climate** for Pittsburgh:

<VarLink hrefKey="narrative.climate.weather_station_url">
    - <Var k="narrative.climate.weather_station_name" />
</VarLink>

It starts from the [Phius climate data set](https://phius.org/climate-data) for Pittsburgh International Airport,
the standard input for a Phius energy model. That data set matches the weather of the 1980s and 1990s, so it is first
brought up to today's observed climate (2011 to 2025), then shifted by the change the climate models project for
2040 to 2059 under the SSP2-4.5 scenario. Solar radiation is unchanged. The full method, and why it was chosen, is on
the [Future Climate](/future-climate/) page.

Pittsburgh's climate today is cold and humid: <Var k="narrative.climate.ashrae_location_name" /> under the energy
code, with long, cloudy winters. By mid-century it behaves more like a mixed-humid climate. January averages just
above freezing, and the average July and August dew point rises to about 66°F, well into the range where summer air
feels muggy and moisture removal becomes a significant part of the cooling load. The Phius Criteria Calculator
already places the site in ASHRAE climate zone <Var k="narrative.user_defined.phius_ashrae_zone" />.

Two parts of the model are deliberately not warmed:

- **Peak heating.** The heating-load design conditions keep their historical values, and the window comfort check
  uses the historical ASHRAE design temperature of <Var k="narrative.climate.ashrae_design_temps" />. Pittsburgh's
  heating design temperature shows no warming trend across recent ASHRAE editions, so holding these values is
  slightly conservative.
- **Phius targets.** The Phius CORE 2024 limits come from the Phius Criteria Calculator, which uses the standard
  climate. They are held as the design targets. In a warmer climate the heating target is slightly easier to meet
  and the cooling target slightly harder, so the heating margins in this report should be read as a little generous
  and the cooling margins as conservative.

The Passive House energy model uses monthly average climate data, which can look different from the hourly weather
data used by some other US energy-modeling programs. The [Resilience](/resilience/) assessment uses the matching
hourly future weather file for its outage simulations.
