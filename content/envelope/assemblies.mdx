These assemblies apply from **As-Drawn onward**. Several differ from the schematic-design narrative, as
noted below. Higher R-values mean less heat loss.

| Assembly | Area (ft²) | Modeled R-value, As-Drawn onward |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Brick-clad wall | 2,613 | 29.9 |
| Wood-clad wall | 1,911 | 32.9 |
| Sloped roof | 2,338 | 32.7 |
| Low-slope roof | 164 | 55.9 |
| Exposed floor | 249 | 33.3 |
| Basement wall | 1,967 | 22.5 |
| Basement slab | 1,862 | 21.1 |

The R-values include the framing. Brick ties and cladding fasteners lower them further; include those
in the final calculations.

### Decisions

- **Sloped roof.** Keep the batt-insulated roof with an interior INTELLO PLUS or comparable smart
  membrane, ADHERO over the plywood and a ventilated cavity under the metal roofing. It is the assembly
  that needs the most attention: large, with unvented-roof moisture requirements. Exterior insulation
  is still worth considering.
- **Basement wall.** We prefer exterior insulation if the team can resolve the cladding and grade
  transition. If the insulation stays inside, add an interior smart membrane and complete the
  foundation moisture checks.
- **Low-slope roof and slab.** Both use foam, against the narrative's foam-free goal. Choose the
  products, then we will recalculate.

The architect coordinates final layers, structural supports and drainage.
