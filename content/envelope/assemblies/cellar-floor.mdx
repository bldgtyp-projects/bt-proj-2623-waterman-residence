As modeled: **R-21.1**, U-0.047 Btu/hr·ft²·°F.

We propose a concrete slab over 4 inches of XPS, with finish floor as desired, departing from the narrative's 3 inches of Comfortboard 110 stone wool, nominal R-12. The modeled XPS assembly has higher thermal resistance, but uses foam plastic. Retain XPS as the current design and modeling basis.

Comfortboard 110 is a possible foam-free alternative. ROCKWOOL's
[below-grade technical bulletin, version 1.2, March 2025](https://d-cdn.rockwool.com/syssiteassets/o2-rockwool/documentation/technical-bulletins/commercial/below-grade-technical-bulletin-v1.1.pdf)
supports Comfortboard 80 and 110 beneath slabs for static live loads up to 100 psf in addition to
typical slab dead loads. It advises against use in high-water-table or flood-prone conditions.
Use of Comfortboard 110 on this project depends on the structural engineer's review and sign-off
on the slab assembly, including loads and deformation, concentrated loads and floor finishes.
Confirm site moisture conditions and coordinate with ROCKWOOL on project-specific suitability.
Retain XPS unless the team selects stone wool following that sign-off; recalculate the assembly
if the insulation changes.

Connect the slab-edge insulation to the wall insulation. Lay a sealed sub-slab vapor barrier, 15 mil per
the narrative, directly above the insulation and below the concrete, and tie it into the wall's air
and moisture control. Provide a capillary break beneath the insulation.

Include a radon-resistant foundation and test after occupancy. See
[EPA guidance](https://www.epa.gov/radon/radon-resistant-construction-basics-and-techniques) for the
soil-gas layer, membrane and vent stack.
