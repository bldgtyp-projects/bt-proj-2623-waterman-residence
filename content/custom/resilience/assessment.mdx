title: "Outage assessment"
---

Alongside the annual energy model, we ran an hourly model of the house through two week-long
power outages in the future climate: a **summer heat wave, July 13 to 19**, and a **winter cold
snap, February 3 to 9**. Outdoor air reaches about **100°F** in the summer week and falls to
about **−5°F** in the winter week. The test follows the
[Phius REVIVE 2024 protocol](https://www.phius.org/phius-revive-2024-standard-document), which
tests what a house does with the power off for a week.

- Heating, cooling, humidification and dehumidification are off during both test periods. Backup
power keeps the ERV running at **15 CFM** (5 CFM per person).
- Windows stay closed in winter and open depending on outdoor temperature in summer.
- Each run covers nine days: one normal day, the seven-day outage, and one day of recovery.
- Results are reported for four floors separately.

#### Phius-REVIVE Criteria

To pass Phius-REVIVE, each zone has to meet both criteria for its season:
| Season | Criterion | REVIVE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Summer | No hours with [Heat Index](https://www.weather.gov/safety/heat-index) at or above 103°F | §6.4.2(a) |
| Summer | No "deadly days", where the day's mean temperature and humidity cross the survivability line | §6.4.2(b) |
| Winter | No hours below 36°F [Standard Effective Temperature (SET)](https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/bookstore/standard-55-thermal-environmental-conditions-for-human-occupancy) | §6.3.2(a) |
| Winter | At most 216 °F-hours of SET below 54°F | §6.3.2(b) |
