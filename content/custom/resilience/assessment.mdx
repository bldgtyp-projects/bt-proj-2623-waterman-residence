In addition to the annual energy model built in the
[Passive House Planning Package (PHPP)](https://passivehouse.com/en/home/planning-tools/), the house will be
assessed with a detailed hourly model for resilience during two test periods:

1. A **summer heat wave** combined with a power outage lasting a full week.
2. A **winter cold snap** combined with a power outage lasting a full week.

These stress tests evaluate whether the house can keep safe and healthy indoor conditions using passive measures
alone: all heating, cooling and ventilation equipment is off for the test period. They follow the
[Phius REVIVE 2024 protocol](https://www.phius.org/phius-revive-2024), including its rules for occupancy, weather and
habitability limits.

- [Phius REVIVE 2024 Standard](https://www.phius.org/phius-revive-2024-standard-document)

Each simulation runs for nine days: one day of normal operation before the event, the seven-day outage, and one day of
recovery after power returns.

### Future-climate weather

On this project the outage tests run on the **mid-century weather file** described on the
[Future Climate](/future-climate/) page, not on historical weather. The winter outage reaches the ASHRAE 10-year
low of <Var k="narrative.user_defined.winter_extreme_F" />°F, held at its historical value. The summer outage reaches
<Var k="narrative.user_defined.future_summer_extreme_F" />°F, the historical 20-year high of
<Var k="narrative.user_defined.present_summer_extreme_F" />°F raised by the projected mid-century summer warming.
The outage weeks fall in early February and mid-July, set by the coldest and hottest weeks of the weather file.
