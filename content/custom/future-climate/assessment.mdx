Our design-phase assessment has three parts, and the future climate enters each one:

| Assessment step | Method | Climate data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual energy demand | PHPP monthly method | Phius data set, vintage correction and mid-century shift |
| Peak heating and cooling loads | PHPP design-day method | Phius design conditions: heating held, cooling shifted |
| Resilience | Phius REVIVE 2024 outage simulation | Shifted hourly weather file; summer extreme raised, winter extreme held |

All five design variants run on the mid-century climate, so the comparison between design options is made in the
climate the house will actually operate in.

### Peak loads: keep the cold, shift the heat

- **Heating: historical values held.** Pittsburgh's ASHRAE 99.6% heating design temperature has ranged between 3.7°F
  and 5.6°F across the last five ASHRAE editions, with no significant trend. Holding the historical heating-load
  conditions is slightly conservative.
- **Cooling: design day shifted.** The Phius cooling-load temperature rises from
  <Var k="narrative.user_defined.present_cooling_load_temp_F" />°F to
  <Var k="narrative.user_defined.future_cooling_load_temp_F" />°F (vintage correction plus the July and August change).
  The Phius data has no cooling-load dew point to shift; the higher monthly dew points still raise the latent load.

### Resilience: the outage weather

The [Phius REVIVE 2024](https://www.phius.org/phius-revive-2024) outage tests reshape the coldest and hottest weeks of
the weather file to reach ASHRAE's extreme values:

| Outage | ASHRAE extreme, present day | Future run |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winter, 7 days | 10-year low, <Var k="narrative.user_defined.winter_extreme_F" />°F | Held at <Var k="narrative.user_defined.winter_extreme_F" />°F |
| Summer, 7 days | 20-year high, <Var k="narrative.user_defined.present_summer_extreme_F" />°F | Raised to <Var k="narrative.user_defined.future_summer_extreme_F" />°F |

Holding the winter value is conservative on purpose: Pittsburgh's 10-year low has already warmed from −13.7°F in the
2009 ASHRAE edition to −7.0°F in 2025. The summer peak has not risen in the historical record, so its increase comes
from the projections. In the NOAA county projections the hottest day of the year warms at about the same rate as the
summer average by mid-century, so raising the summer extreme by the summer change is a fair estimate.

### What is not in scope

Decided with the owners in October 2026: one future case only; no 2080s or high-emission runs for now; the assembly
moisture review stays on present-day weather. Those remain available as later sensitivities.
