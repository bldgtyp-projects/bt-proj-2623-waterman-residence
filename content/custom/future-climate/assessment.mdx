The climate inputs enter the studies differently:

| Assessment | Current weather basis |
| --- | --- |
| Annual [PHPP](https://passivehouse.com/04_phpp/04_phpp.htm) energy model | Modified monthly Pittsburgh International climate, adjusted for site elevation |
| Peak heating | Historical heating-load conditions, adjusted for site elevation |
| Peak cooling | Modified PHPP cooling conditions |
| Hourly outage model | Future hourly Pittsburgh International file; [REVIVE](https://www.phius.org/phius-revive-2024-standard-document) extremes from Allegheny County Airport |

### Heating and cooling design

Heating loads use historical winter conditions. Milder average winters don't remove cold snaps. Size
the equipment from room-by-room loads and the manufacturer's low-temperature capacity.

The future climate raises the cooling design temperature from about **77.7°F to 82.0°F** and raises
summer dew points, which adds to the moisture the equipment must remove.

### Outage weather

The outage weeks are **February 3 to 9** and **July 13 to 19**. Temperature extremes come from the
nearer **Allegheny County Airport**: **−6.2°F** in winter, and about **100°F** in summer after the
projected warming ([ASHRAE climatic design data](https://ashrae-meteo.info/v3.0/)). Summer humidity
follows the future weather file, which is a conservative assumption.

Neither the annual nor the outage model checks whether walls and roofs stay dry. That needs its own
moisture review.
