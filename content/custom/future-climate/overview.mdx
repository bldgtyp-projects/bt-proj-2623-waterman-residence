title: "Why use a future climate?"
dek: "Why this house is assessed against mid-century weather as well as today's."
---

A house built now will operate through a changing climate. At the owners' request, the annual energy model
uses projected average weather for **2040 to 2059** under the **SSP2-4.5** scenario.

SSP2-4.5 is the intermediate emissions scenario in the
[IPCC Sixth Assessment Report](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/summary-for-policymakers/):
emissions stay near today's levels until about mid-century, then decline.

The projected monthly changes come from the
[ORNL Cross-Model future weather dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/10719179), checked against
[NOAA county projections](https://cris.climate.gov/datasets/cris-zonal-statistics-by-county-1).
They add about **2.8°F** of annual warming to a recent typical year, for an annual mean of about
**57.1°F** at the site.

The outage study uses the same future weather, with heat-wave and cold-snap extremes set by the
[Phius REVIVE 2024 standard](https://www.phius.org/phius-revive-2024-standard-document), a method for
testing how a house holds up in a power outage. See [How the weather is used](#future-climate-assessment).
