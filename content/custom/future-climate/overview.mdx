Standard energy models use typical weather drawn from past decades. A house built in 2027 will run most of its
life in a warmer climate, and its heat pumps and ventilation equipment will be sized, bought and replaced against that
climate. At the owners' request, every energy result in this report is therefore calculated against a projected
**mid-century climate** rather than the standard Phius climate data.

The method was proposed by the owners, reviewed together in October 2026, and confirmed before any modeling began.
In short: take a recent, observed year of Pittsburgh weather and shift its temperature and humidity by the amount the
climate models project for the middle of the century.

### Scenario and time horizon

The central case is **<Var k="narrative.user_defined.future_scenario" />**, the middle-of-the-road emissions scenario
used by the IPCC and the US National Climate Assessment. The 2040 to 2059 period covers the main years the owners will
live in the house and the expected life of the heat pumps and ventilation equipment. It also matches the horizon NYC's
[Climate Resiliency Design Guidelines](https://facilities.weill.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/downloads/nyc_climate_resiliency_design_guidelines_v4-0.pdf)
assign to mechanical equipment.

| Case | Role | Annual mean change | June to August change | Heating degree days (65°F) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Present day | Baseline | 0 | 0 | 5,167 |
| SSP2-4.5, 2040 to 2059 | Central case, used in this report | +2.8°F | +3.1°F | 4,627 |
| SSP2-4.5, 2080 to 2099 | Later sensitivity, not run | +4.6°F | +5.7°F | 4,283 |
| SSP5-8.5, 2080 to 2099 | Later sensitivity, not run | +8.8°F | +10.2°F | 3,433 |

Temperature changes are from the [ORNL Cross-Model future weather files](https://zenodo.org/records/10719179) for
Allegheny County; degree days are from the [NOAA CRIS county projections](https://cris.climate.gov/datasets/cris-zonal-statistics-by-county-1).
The two sources agree within 0.2°F on annual mean temperature. Dew point rises about as fast as air temperature, so
summers bring more moisture as well as more heat.

At mid-century the scenarios are still close together: SSP2-4.5 and the higher-emission pathways land within about
1°F of each other by 2050 and spread apart only after that. The later periods are kept as separate, labelled
sensitivities rather than blended into one file, so every result in this report traces to one stated climate.
